## Key structural facts
- Reading and Listening are **two-stage adaptive** (router module -> lower OR upper module).
  Writing and Speaking are **linear** (everyone on a form gets the same tasks).
- Reading/Listening item counts INCLUDE non-scored (pilot) items. Reading 50 items -> 35 raw points.
  Listening 47 items -> 35 raw points.
- Max points per item: Reading 1, Listening 1, Build a Sentence 1,
  Write an Email 5, Write for an Academic Discussion 5, Listen and Repeat 5, Take an Interview 5.
- Speaking/Writing constructed responses are AI scored (NLP/ML: fluency, coherence, grammar).

## Reading stimulus specs
- Allowable item types: Complete the Words; Read in Daily Life (**2-item and 3-item sets**); Read an Academic Passage.
- Easier texts 15–50 words; more complex texts extend to ~200 words.
- Complexity calibrated with TextEvaluator: medium 800–1100 (approx. grade 10–11),
  high 1100–1360+ (approx. grade 11–12+). Flesch-Kincaid: medium 7.4–10.5, high 10.5–20.0.
- All texts remain visible throughout the task (test taker can refer back while answering).
- Stimuli formats: sign, menu, email, social media post, ... through extended academic passages.
- Language use context: Academic and Social Interpersonal.

## Listening stimulus specs
- Audio ranges from brief utterances (**no longer than six stressed syllables**) to extended
  monologic speech of **up to 250 words**. Intermediate-length audio typically **35–100 words**.
- **Recordings are played once.**
- Each spoken input accompanied by a **visual representation of the speaker(s)**.
- Items presented with printed stems + multiple-choice options, individually or in sets.
- AI-generated audio and sometimes human voice actors; balanced regional accents
  (U.S./Canadian, Australian, British) and gendered voices. Natural pace.
- Includes idiomatic/colloquial expressions; rhetorical patterns (compare/contrast, cause-effect).

## Writing specs
- Build a Sentence: reconstruct a range of sentence structures (Social Interpersonal context), 1 pt.
- Write an Email: appropriate multi-sentence text (Academic Navigational context), 5 pts.
- Write for an Academic Discussion: academic paragraph presenting a clear, well-supported
  argument using varied grammar and vocabulary (Academic context), 5 pts.
- Prompts may include contextual cues: audience, purpose, situation.

## Speaking specs
- Language use context: **Academic Navigational** for both task types.
- Listen and Repeat: repetition items **increase in length and syntactic complexity** across the
  task — start with short single independent clauses, progress to compound verbs, dependent
  clauses, relative clauses, complex nominal phrases.
- Take an Interview: elicits descriptions, explanations, opinions, suggestions, predictions,
  narrations; difficulty increases across the task.
- Each task begins with a **scenario introduction delivered both aurally and in print**.
- Visual elements: schematic images, maps, forms, or photos of speakers.
- Topics accessible to a general audience: life sciences, history, business, campus navigation.
- Vocabulary avoids overly technical terms; incorporates natural spoken features such as contractions.
